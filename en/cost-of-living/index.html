Rent: the biggest variable

Rent is usually 40% or more of a foreigner's budget. According to the Ministry of the Interior's rent statistics for the first half of 2026, the median Taipei studio (獨立套房) rents for NT$15,000 a month and a room in a shared flat (分租雅房) for NT$9,800. Neihu, Songshan, Da'an and Xinyi are the most expensive districts at NT$18,000–19,500 for a studio, while Wenshan, Wanhua, Beitou and Shilin are the cheapest. These figures come from rent-subsidy leases, so newer, furnished, foreigner-friendly places in the center typically cost 20–30% more.

New Taipei (Banqiao, Zhonghe, Xindian, Sanchong) is the classic way to save while commuting into Taipei on the same MRT system and transit pass. In Taichung and Kaohsiung, a studio around NT$8,000–10,000 is common, and a whole apartment costs about what a studio does in central Taipei.

  • Deposit: legally capped at two months' rent and refundable at the end of the lease.
  • Agent fee: up to one month's rent split between landlord and tenant; tenants usually pay half a month. Renting directly from the landlord (屋主直租) avoids it.
  • Electricity: many landlords charge NT$5–6 per kWh on a sub-meter instead of the Taipower rate. In summer, running air-conditioning can add NT$1,000–2,000 a month. Check the rate before signing.
  • Rent tax deduction: tax residents can deduct up to NT$180,000 a year of rent, so ask whether the landlord allows you to register the lease.

Transport

Monthly TPASS passes give unlimited rides on the MRT, city buses and trains within a region. The Keelung–Taipei–New Taipei–Taoyuan pass costs NT$1,200 and includes the first 30 minutes of each YouBike ride. Taichung's regional pass (Taichung, Changhua, Nantou, Miaoli) costs NT$699 for Taichung residents or NT$999 otherwise, with cheaper city-only passes, and Kaohsiung's city pass for MRT, light rail and buses is NT$399. Prices are set by local governments and change from time to time. Outside Taipei, many residents ride a scooter instead: count roughly NT$1,500–2,000 a month for fuel, insurance and parking, plus an international or Taiwan license.

Food

Eating out is cheap and normal in Taiwan. A lunch box (便當) or bowl of noodles costs NT$90–150, a café meal NT$250–400, and a dinner at a mid-range restaurant NT$400–800 per person. Imported groceries such as cheese, cereal and wine cost much more than at home, so a Western-style diet raises your bill quickly. Traditional markets and chains like PX Mart are the cheapest places to shop.

Health insurance (NHI)

Taiwan's National Health Insurance is one of the main reasons living here is affordable. A clinic visit costs about NT$50–200 in co-payments.

  • Employed with an ARC: enrolled from your first day; you pay 30% of the premium through payroll (about NT$460–1,600 a month depending on salary).
  • No employer (students, spouses, Gold Card holders without a job): you must wait until you have lived in Taiwan for 6 months, then pay about NT$826 a month as a Category 6 insured person. Leaving Taiwan for more than 30 days in a row restarts the waiting period.
  • Before NHI starts: buy private travel or expat insurance. NHI is mandatory once you are eligible.

FAQ

Is NT$50,000 a month enough to live in Taipei?

Yes for a single person. After Labor Insurance and NHI, NT$50,000 leaves about NT$48,100, which covers a moderate studio lifestyle in Taipei (about NT$36,000) with NT$10,000 or more to save. Families with children will need considerably more.

Which city is best for foreigners on a budget?

Kaohsiung and Taichung offer the lowest rents among the big cities, good MRT or bus networks, and plenty of English-language community groups. Taipei has the most jobs and the most English signage, but rents are 40–75% higher.

What upfront costs should I plan for?

About 3.5 months of rent for move-in, NT$3,000–6,000 for a scooter license and helmet or an EasyCard top-up, a SIM card and ARC fees (NT$1,000 per year of validity), and possibly basic furniture if the apartment is unfurnished.

How accurate is this calculator?

It uses 2026 median rents and current insurance and transport prices, then applies typical spending patterns. Your own costs will vary with neighborhood, diet and travel. Treat it as a planning range, not a quote.
