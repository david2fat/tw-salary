Anniversary vs calendar-year systems

Under the anniversary system, leave is granted each time you reach a service milestone counted from your start date. Under the calendar-year system, the employer uses January to December and prorates each service year's leave by how many of its days fall in that calendar year. The Ministry of Labor requires that a calendar-year employee never ends up with fewer days in total than the anniversary system would give, and any shortfall must be made up when you leave.

Unused leave

  • End of the year: unused days are paid out, or carried over by agreement for one more year and paid if still unused.
  • Leaving the job: whether you resign or are laid off, unused days are paid as wages.
  • You choose the dates: the employee schedules annual leave; the employer can only ask to adjust it for urgent business needs.

FAQ

Do part-time workers get annual leave?

Yes. Part-timers earn leave by seniority too, with hours prorated by their average weekly hours compared with full-time hours.

Do foreigners get the same annual leave?

Yes. Article 38 applies to all employees covered by the Labor Standards Act, regardless of nationality.
