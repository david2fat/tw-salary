The 183-day rule: resident or non-resident?

Taiwan decides your tax status one calendar year at a time. If you are a foreign national without Taiwan household registration, you are a tax resident for the year when you spend 183 days or more in Taiwan between January 1 and December 31. Otherwise you are a non-resident. Days are counted from your entry and exit stamps, and the count restarts every January, so a person who arrives on July 15 is usually a non-resident for their first year even with a long-term contract.

Non-resident tax: a flat 18% (or 6%)

As a non-resident, your employer withholds tax from every payment and that withholding is final, with no personal exemption, no standard deduction and no salary deduction. The rate is 18%. If a monthly payment is at or below 1.5 times the minimum wage (NT$29,500 × 1.5 = NT$44,250 in 2026), the rate drops to 6%. Bonuses are withheld the same way.

Because residents get roughly NT$464,000 of tax-free allowances (exemption NT$101,000 + standard deduction NT$136,000 + salary deduction NT$227,000) and a starting rate of 5%, being a non-resident is far more expensive for almost everyone. On NT$60,000 a month, a full year as a non-resident costs NT$129,600 in tax, while a resident pays about NT$12,800.

Your first year: overpaying, then getting a refund

Employers do not know in advance whether you will reach 183 days, so new arrivals are often withheld at 18% for the first months. Once you pass 183 days in the year, ask HR to switch you to resident withholding. The extra tax already taken is not lost: you file a resident return the following May (or a departure return if you leave), and the difference is refunded to your Taiwan bank account. Use the comparison box in the calculator to see how much is at stake.

If you are leaving Taiwan for good, you must settle your tax for the year before you go. Your employer or you file a departure return with the National Taxation Bureau, and you may be stopped at the airport if taxes are unpaid above the legal threshold.

Labor Insurance, NHI and pension for foreigners

  • Labor Insurance: foreign employees with an ARC are enrolled from day one. You pay 20% of 11.5% of your insured salary (capped at NT$45,800), about NT$1,053 a month at most.
  • Employment Insurance: generally not for foreigners, except spouses of Taiwan citizens who hold residence permits, so the calculator leaves it out.
  • National Health Insurance: employed ARC holders join immediately with no waiting period. You pay 30% of 5.17% of your insured amount, plus the same for each dependent you add.
  • Labor Pension (6%): required for foreigners with permanent residency (APRC) or married to a Taiwan citizen. Other foreign employees are usually not covered.

Tax break for foreign professionals and Gold Card holders

Under the Act for the Recruitment and Employment of Foreign Professionals, a foreign special professional (including Employment Gold Card holders) who comes to Taiwan to work for the first time, is a tax resident, and earns more than NT$3 million a year in salary can exclude 50% of the amount above NT$3 million from taxable income for up to five years. Overseas income is also left out of the alternative minimum tax base. You must apply when filing your return. The government published draft amendments in early 2026 to widen the eligible fields; the 50% / NT$3 million / 5-year terms are unchanged.

FAQ

Do my days count if I leave Taiwan for a holiday?

Only days physically in Taiwan count toward 183. Trips abroad pause the count. The day of arrival and the day of departure are each generally counted as a day in Taiwan.

I work remotely for a foreign company while living in Taiwan. Do I pay tax here?

Pay for work physically done in Taiwan is Taiwan-source income even if a foreign company pays it abroad. If you stay more than 90 days in the year it is taxable here. Self-reporting is your responsibility since there is no local employer to withhold.

Is my foreign investment income taxed?

Overseas income is not subject to regular Taiwan income tax. Residents with more than NT$1 million of overseas income may owe alternative minimum tax (AMT) if total basic income exceeds the exemption amount.

Why is my payslip different from this calculator?

Your employer may insure you at a different grade, pay tax-free allowances, deduct welfare fund or group insurance, or use the withholding table instead of 5%. The calculator gives a close estimate, not your exact payslip.

How do I file my tax return?

Filing runs May 1–31. Foreigners with an ARC can file online through the Ministry of Finance eTax portal using their ARC number and a tax certificate, or in person at any National Taxation Bureau office, which usually has English-speaking staff during filing season.
